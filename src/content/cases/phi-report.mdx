## Context

Denti.AI Voice Perio lets dentists fill in a periodontal chart by voice during an
exam. They don't have to type anything or call in an assistant. A perio chart is the
standard dental record of gum health: pocket depths, bleeding, mobility and
furcation, for every tooth.

<VideoLoop src="/media/phi-report/voice-perio" caption="Voice Perio in a real exam: each spoken command lands in the chart as it's said" />

When the chart is complete, the dentist also gets a **PHI (Periodontal Health Index)
report**. It's an AI-generated summary of the patient's periodontal condition, key
findings, risk factors and treatment recommendations, and its main job is to help the
patient understand the state of their own teeth.

By the time of this redesign, Voice Perio had completed **over 1 million perio
charts** in clinics across the USA and Canada. Denti.AI works directly with
individual clinics, so feedback reaches the team constantly. Once the feedback on a
feature crosses a threshold, the team reworks it. This case is that stage of the
PHI report's life.

## The problem

The task was to fix the report's key issues and make it more educational for
patients, **while preserving the existing format**.

Here's what clinics told us:

- "Too little space for info"
- "Too much blank space"
- "We want to see suppuration and plaque scores too"

They also said the report was impossible to use on phones and tablets, and they
wanted it to look just as good in print, as a generated PDF. On top of that, they
asked for two new things: bleeding points for every tooth (6 sites × 32 teeth =
**192 points**) and a stage visualization for the whole mouth.

My own review added one more problem: the layout itself felt clumsy and unbalanced.

So the report needed more data, the same size, and a layout that could adapt to any medium.

<Figure clear src="/media/phi-report/before" caption="The original report" />

## Decision 1: a selector, not a scale

The old report showed oral health on a horizontal six-step scale. On closer look,
it wasn't a real scale at all. It was a selector with six fixed values, and laying
it out horizontally only took up space without adding meaning.

I replaced it with a compact radio group. The six health stages were the same
stages as the new full-mouth visualization, so I merged the two into one block. The
block works just as well vertically as horizontally, which turned out to matter for
the layout later.

<Figure clear src="/media/phi-report/scale-evolution" caption="From scale to selector: the original scale, the first radio-group version, and the final block" />

<Figure wide src="/media/phi-report/scale-states" caption="The final block in all six states. The patient's stage is highlighted and carries their score" />

## Decision 2: put the data inside the tooth chart

There was no obvious way to add 192 bleeding points to a report that was already
short on space. Any new block would make the report bigger, and that was exactly
what clinics didn't want.

So I started with the tooth chart itself. I redrew it so the teeth look like actual
teeth, which made it easier for patients to read. The new shapes left room for the
data, so I placed all six bleeding sites per tooth and every pocket depth of 4 mm or
more directly on the chart. The block didn't grow at all.

The new report has a more confident layout and higher information density. Every
new element found its place, and the overall footprint stayed the same.

## Decision 3: one structure for any medium

The report is built from modules that rearrange without changing their content. The
same structure becomes a wide desktop layout, a single mobile column or a printable
page, and it can adapt to any other format the same way.

## Results

After two weeks with the new design, **84% of clinics stayed with it** without any
prompting. Only 16% switched back to the old version. Over the same period, PHI
usage grew by 2% and Voice Perio usage by 5%.

The report itself now carries noticeably more useful information per unit of
space: 192 bleeding points, suppuration and plaque scores, and a stage view of the
whole mouth, all within the original footprint.

That validated the approach, and the new report design was rolled out across all
Denti.AI products.
